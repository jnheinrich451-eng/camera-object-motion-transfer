Recover, Retarget, Guide

Camera and object motion transfer with a frozen video model

John Zeng · supervisor Dr Jing Zhang

A reference video supplies a camera motion and a subject motion; a text prompt supplies a new subject in a new scene. The motion visible in the image mixes the two with the scene's depth, so copying image-plane displacement cannot separate the camera from the subject, and a displacement measured in the source scene need not fit the geometry of the new one. This work builds the motion target in the target scene before any video is generated, and corrects a frozen video generator toward it. Nothing is trained.

How it works

Three stages, one frozen generator

01

Recover

The reference video is lifted to a camera trajectory, a subject path and a ground plane in one frame and one scale, with a frozen 3D tracker. Static background and moving subject are told apart by a drift model, the subject is masked without a human prompt, and a clip whose camera, subject or support surface cannot be recovered is refused.

02

Retarget

A proxy of the target category is placed where the source subject stood, facing its heading on its ground; a depth control built from that placement drives a depth-conditioned image generator; the anchor image is selected automatically and reconstructed in three dimensions.

03

Guide

The anchor's own content is rendered along the recovered camera path, and for rigid targets the object along the subject's path. Inside a window of noise levels, the frozen generator's clean estimate is corrected toward that render wherever a mask marks it reliable. One encode, no gradient, sixteen of fifty steps.

Results

Fifteen test pairs, against the same generator without guidance

Fifteen pairs passed a screening fixed before any video was generated: thirteen MiraData clips and two DAVIS clips, each with a human or vehicle target set by its caption. Every pair was generated once (seed 0, 21 frames) by the backbone, Wan2.1 image-to-video, and by the same backbone with our correction. The two primary metrics were fixed before scoring: the camera's rotation error against the reference camera (degrees) and the subject's on-screen path error relative to its own first frame (pixels). Lower is better for both.

How we got here

What was tried, what was kept, what was set aside

How the method got here, in order, including what was tried and set aside. Each step says what the evidence was.

  1. 01 · kept

    Lift the reference to three dimensions

    A frozen 3D tracker gives tracks, depth and a camera that is consistent inside one of its encoding windows; an external camera estimate is archived beside it with the scale ratio that ties the two. A drift model fitted on the static background tells static from moving.

    evidence The tracker's camera degrades across window boundaries by about four times; measured on the calibration clips, and the reason a third camera is solved later.

  2. 02 · kept

    A branch for clips where the camera follows the subject

    When the camera travels with the subject, the subject is nearly still in the shared frame and the ordinary proposal degenerates. A detector finds the quiet, compact blob of the subject and a separate branch composes its path with the camera.

    evidence The degeneracy was measured on the follow-camera calibration clip before the branch was designed.

  3. 03 · rejected

    Per-frame drift correction of the camera

    A per-frame scale correction of the camera was considered as a default.

    evidence On the calibration clips the drift it would remove is present on some and absent on others, so applying it throughout would introduce the error it is meant to remove.

  4. 04 · kept

    The anchor by depth-conditioned generation

    A category proxy is placed where the source subject stood, facing its heading on the recovered ground; a hybrid control depth drives a depth-conditioned image generator; the anchor is selected by a fixed rule with a facing test.

    evidence Automatic selection delivered all fifteen test anchors with one unclear subject, read by eye before any score.

  5. 05 · tried, not adopted

    Road-layout conditioning of the anchor

    On the camera-following clip the anchors placed the car on roads that did not afford its turn. The road's boundaries were added as a second spatial condition beside the depth.

    evidence The road's shape was kept on inspected examples, at the cost of appearance and generalisation, and the best inputs were traced by hand; the line was closed and Stage II left unchanged.

  6. 06 · built, set aside

    Attention-readout guidance

    The first guidance read a displacement field from one transformer block's attention and optimised the latent toward the displacement the geometry predicts, with an epipolar term where coverage was low.

    evidence On generated video the loss was satisfied by content rather than by motion. The readout stayed as an instrument: it was gated against CoTracker3 tracks on the source clips.

  7. 07 · kept

    The render-target correction

    The anchor's own content is rendered along the recovered camera, and the object along its path for rigid targets. Inside a window of noise levels the clean estimate is pulled toward the render where a mask holds; no gradient, one encode.

    evidence Camera rotation error falls on 15 of 15 test pairs (median 3.59 to 1.08 degrees), significant after Holm correction.

  8. 08 · designed, not built

    A geometry refresh from the denoised estimate

    Newly revealed background would be re-lifted from the decoded estimate at an anchor frame, so coverage would not decay over the video.

    evidence The rule that chooses the noise levels from the estimate's depth error returned an empty set: where the depth is usable the content has already changed. So coverage decays, and the late-frame distortion the review saw is left as future work.

  9. 09 · changed

    Which camera the generator consumes

    The development set ran on the external camera, bridged into the source gauge by a depth ratio at one frame and a second scalar read at the object. The final configuration solves the camera from the static tracks: perspective-n-point per frame, then a bundle adjustment, in the gauge of the lifted scene, so no bridge is needed.

    evidence Held-out static points reproject within a fraction of a patch through the refined cameras; the same test is the camera check every test pair passed.

  10. 10 · kept, tested

    The sampler's details

    The multistep scheduler's corrector is disabled at the corrected steps; the mask is soft at its edges; the sky at infinity is inside the mask with a rotation-only target.

    evidence In the guidance ablations the hard mask is worse on the subject's path on 5 of 5 pairs, the sky out is worse on the camera on 4 of 5, gamma between 0.25 and 1 is indistinguishable on five pairs, and a first-order sampler keeps the camera but hurts the subject.

Notes

What this does not do, and what it is built on

  • How an articulated subject moves its own body is not transferred: the human arm renders no object, so once the camera is held the model's own gait is carried along with it, and the subject's path error rises on 9 of 12 human pairs.
  • The correction costs appearance: both CLIP scores fall under guidance on nearly every pair, and the cost does not scale with how much the correction has to do.
  • Frame-zero coverage decays over the video and is not replenished, so later frames can distort where the render no longer covers them.
  • A reference whose camera fails the check is refused rather than guessed; the test set is fifteen pairs at one seed.
